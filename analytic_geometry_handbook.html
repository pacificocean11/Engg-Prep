This compact handbook is specifically designed for Mechanical Engineering FE Exam candidates. It provides detailed, highly-visual, and essential formulas for Analytic Geometry, bridging the gap between a dense textbook and the sparse official FE Reference Handbook.

1. Coordinate Systems

Rectangular (Cartesian) Coordinates

Coordinates are given by \((x, y, z)\). Distance between two points \(P_1(x_1, y_1, z_1)\) and \(P_2(x_2, y_2, z_2)\):

\( d = \sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2 + (z_2 - z_1)^2} \)

Polar Coordinates (2D)

Coordinates are given by \((r, \theta)\).

  • \( x = r \cos\theta \)
  • \( y = r \sin\theta \)
  • \( r = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2} \)
  • \( \theta = \arctan(y/x) \)
Pro Tip for the FE Exam

Always watch your units for angles! The FE Exam will try to trick you by mixing degrees and radians when converting from rectangular to polar coordinates.

2. Straight Lines

The general equation of a straight line in 2D is \(Ax + By + C = 0\).

Form Equation When to use
Slope-Intercept \(y = mx + b\) When slope \(m\) and y-intercept \(b\) are known.
Point-Slope \(y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)\) When one point and slope are known.
Two-Point \(\frac{y - y_1}{x - x_1} = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}\) When two points on the line are known.

Distance from a Point to a Line

The perpendicular distance \(d\) from a point \((x_1, y_1)\) to the line \(Ax + By + C = 0\) is:

\( d = \frac{|Ax_1 + By_1 + C|}{\sqrt{A^2 + B^2}} \)

3. Conic Sections

The general form of a conic section is: \(Ax^2 + Bxy + Cy^2 + Dx + Ey + F = 0\).

Discriminant Test

You can instantly identify the conic geometry by evaluating the discriminant \(B^2 - 4AC\):

  • If \(B^2 - 4AC < 0\), it's an Ellipse (or circle if \(A=C\) and \(B=0\)).
  • If \(B^2 - 4AC = 0\), it's a Parabola.
  • If \(B^2 - 4AC > 0\), it's a Hyperbola.

Circle

Standard equation with center \((h, k)\) and radius \(r\):

\( (x - h)^2 + (y - k)^2 = r^2 \)

Parabola

Standard equation opening vertically (vertex at \((h, k)\)):

\( (x - h)^2 = 4p(y - k) \)

Focus: \((h, k+p)\)
Directrix: \(y = k-p\)

[ Diagram: Parabola Focus/Directrix ]

Ellipse

Standard equation (horizontal major axis):

\( \frac{(x-h)^2}{a^2} + \frac{(y-k)^2}{b^2} = 1 \)

Eccentricity: \(e = \frac{c}{a} < 1\) where \(c^2 = a^2 - b^2\)

[ Diagram: Ellipse Geometry ]

Hyperbola

Standard equation (horizontal transverse axis):

\( \frac{(x-h)^2}{a^2} - \frac{(y-k)^2}{b^2} = 1 \)

Eccentricity: \(e = \frac{c}{a} > 1\) where \(c^2 = a^2 + b^2\)
